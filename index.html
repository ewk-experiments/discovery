ENERGY / BRIGHTNESS
How loud and how bright the track is over time. Energy (black) shows overall volume intensity. Brightness (red) tracks how much high-frequency content is present — higher means sharper, more present sound.
SPECTRAL FLUX — TEXTURE CHANGE RATE
How quickly the sound's character is changing at each moment. Tall bars mean the texture is shifting fast — new sounds entering, transitions happening. Low bars mean a steady, consistent sound.
CHROMAGRAM — HARMONIC CONTENT
A piano-roll view of which musical notes are present over time. Each row is a note (C through B), and brighter cells mean that note is louder at that moment. Patterns here reveal the track's harmonic language.
CHORD PROGRESSION
The estimated chord at each bar of the song. Each block is a chord — the color represents the root note, and the label shows the full chord name. This is the harmonic skeleton of the track.
ONSET DENSITY — TRANSIENT ACTIVITY
How many distinct sound events (hits, plucks, attacks) happen each second. Tall bars mean lots of activity — busy, dense sections. The red dashed line shows the average. Peaks usually align with drops or climaxes.
MFCC TIMBRAL FINGERPRINT
A fingerprint of the track's tonal color — what makes it sound warm, metallic, breathy, or hollow. Each bar represents a different dimension of timbre. Think of it as the DNA of how the track actually sounds, independent of pitch or rhythm.
SPECTRAL ROLLOFF / BANDWIDTH
Rolloff (black) shows where most of the sound's energy lives — the frequency ceiling. Bandwidth (red) shows how spread out the frequencies are. Wide bandwidth = full, rich sound. Narrow = focused, minimal.
ZERO CROSSING RATE — PERCUSSIVE VS TONAL
How rapidly the audio signal crosses silence. High rates mean noisy, percussive content (drums, clicks, static). Low rates mean smooth, tonal content (pads, bass, sustained notes). Shows the rhythmic vs melodic balance.
LOUDNESS MAP — PSEUDO-LUFS PER SECTION
How loud each section of the track is in LUFS (the standard for measuring perceived loudness). Darker blocks are louder. This reveals the dynamic journey — where the track breathes and where it hits hardest.
SPECTRAL CONTRAST — 7-BAND ANALYSIS
The difference between peaks and valleys in 7 frequency bands (sub-bass through air) across each section. High contrast means clear, defined sounds. Low contrast means dense, muddy, or heavily compressed. Each column is a section, each row is a frequency band.
